\begin{abstract}
% TODO(viết cuối cùng, sau khi có bảng số liệu thật)
LLM-based multi-agent systems can operate AutoML platforms, but they
typically spend their training-job budget blindly. We equip a production
LangGraph multi-agent AutoML system (HAgent, built on the open-source
HAutoML platform) with a deliberately lightweight learned world model: a
calibrated deep-ensemble outcome predictor over campaign configurations,
cross-entropy planning in configuration space, budget-annealed Thompson
batch planning, and an outcome-space surprise signal that triggers
campaign replanning on the real agent. On six real tabular datasets with
real scikit-learn training jobs, we measure the effect of each mechanism
at equal job budgets, and separately benchmark the platform's five HPO
strategies at two data scales, showing that strategy rankings invert with
scale. We release all code, checkpoints, and per-run records.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A lightweight world model for AutoML operations: a Gaussian-NLL
        outcome head with dataset meta-features and explicit
        algorithm-meta crossing terms, deep-ensembled for calibrated
        uncertainty (coverage/ECE reported).
  \item Planning in campaign-configuration space: true CEM with a
        model-subset (Bernoulli) dimension, and a budget-annealed Thompson
        batch planner used for surprise-triggered campaign extension on
        the production agent.
  \item An honest two-layer evaluation: (i) agent-level conditions
        (WM-off / WM-on / WM+replanning) with real LLMs and real
        in-process scikit-learn jobs at equal budgets; (ii) a
        library-level HPO benchmark across two data scales (150 rows to
        581k rows / 251\,MB) in which strategy rankings invert.
\end{enumerate}
